Class 12 Chapter 5: Molecular Basis of Inheritance

1. Structure of DNA, Packaging & Chargaff's Rules

Polynucleotide Chain & Bonds

• Nucleotide: Nitrogenous base + pentose sugar + phosphate group.
• N-glycosidic linkage: Links nitrogenous base to C1' of pentose sugar.
• Phosphoester bond: Links phosphate to C5'-OH of nucleoside.
• 3'–5' Phosphodiester linkage: Connects adjacent nucleotides.
• Polarity: One end has free 5'-phosphate, other has free 3'-OH.

B-DNA Double Helix Dimensions

• Watson & Crick (1953): Based on X-ray diffraction of Wilkins & Franklin.
• Anti-parallel chains: 5'→3' and 3'→5'.
• Base pairing: A=T (2 H-bonds) and G≡C (3 H-bonds).
• Pitch of helix: 3.4 nm with ~10 bp per turn.
• Distance between adjacent bp: 0.34 nm (3.4 Å).
• Chargaff's Rule: %A = %T and %G = %C; (A+G) = (T+C).

Chromatin & Nucleosome Packaging

• DNA length in human cell: 6.6 × 109 bp × 0.34 nm ≈ 2.2 metres.
• Histone Octamer: Positively charged basic proteins rich in lysine & arginine (two each of H2A, H2B, H3, H4) + H1 linker.
• Nucleosome: ~200 bp DNA wrapped around octamer ('beads-on-string').
• Euchromatin: Loosely packed, stains light, transcriptionally active.
• Heterochromatin: Densely packed, stains dark, inactive.

2. Landmark Experiments Proving DNA is Genetic Material & Replicates Semiconservatively

Experiment & Year Organism / System Methodology & Key Reagents Definitive Conclusion
Griffith (1928) Streptococcus pneumoniae in mice Heat-killed S strain + live R strain injected into mice → mice died; live S recovered Discovered 'Transforming Principle'; genetic material transferred from dead S to live R
Avery, MacLeod, McCarty (1933–44) In vitro transformation of S. pneumoniae Purified proteins, RNA, DNA from S cells; digested with Protease, RNase, and DNase Only DNase inhibited transformation; proved DNA is the transforming genetic principle
Hershey & Chase (1952) Bacteriophage T2 infecting E. coli Phages labeled with 32P (DNA) and 35S (protein coat); blending + centrifugation Unequivocal proof that DNA enters bacteria and acts as genetic material (32P in pellet, 35S in supernatant)
Meselson & Stahl (1958) Escherichia coli Grown in 15NH4Cl → shifted to 14NH4Cl; CsCl equilibrium density gradient centrifugation Proved semiconservative replication (Gen 1: 100% hybrid 15N/14N; Gen 2: 50% hybrid + 50% light)
Taylor & colleagues (1958) Vicia faba (broad bean) Root tip dividing chromosomes labeled with radioactive tritiated thymidine (3H-thymidine) Proved semiconservative replication occurs in eukaryotic chromosomes

3. Transcription Machinery, RNA Polymerases & Post-Transcriptional Processing

Transcription Unit

• Promoter: 5'-upstream binding site for RNA polymerase; defines template vs coding strands.
• Template strand: 3'→5' polarity, transcribed into complementary RNA.
• Coding strand: 5'→3' polarity, same sequence as RNA (except T for U).
• Terminator: 3'-downstream site ending transcription.

Eukaryotic RNA Polymerases

• RNA Polymerase I: Transcribes 28S, 18S, and 5.8S rRNAs.
• RNA Polymerase II: Transcribes hnRNA (precursor of mRNA).
• RNA Polymerase III: Transcribes tRNA, 5S rRNA, and snRNAs.
• Prokaryotes: Single RNA polymerase handles all RNAs (uses σ factor to initiate, ρ factor to terminate).

Processing of hnRNA into mRNA

• Splicing: Non-coding introns are excised, coding exons are ligated.
• Capping: Methyl guanosine triphosphate (m7Gppp) added to 5'-end.
• Tailing: 200–300 adenylate residues added to 3'-end in template-independent manner.
• Fully processed transcript is transported to cytoplasm as mature mRNA.

4. Genetic Code, Translation & The Lac Operon Regulation

Salient Features of Genetic Code

• Triplet: 61 sense codons + 3 stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA).

• Degenerate: Most amino acids are coded by more than one codon.

• Unambiguous & Specific: One codon codes for only one amino acid.

• Universal: Same codon codes for same amino acid across almost all taxa (UUU = Phe from bacteria to human; minor exceptions in mitochondria and protozoa).

• AUG Dual Role: Codes for Methionine and acts as initiator codon.

• tRNA Adapter: Clover-leaf 2D structure, compact inverted L 3D structure; anticodon loop pairs with mRNA; amino acid acceptor stem binds amino acid.

Regulation of Gene Expression: Lac Operon

• Elucidated by François Jacob (geneticist) and Jacques Monod (biochemist).

• i gene (inhibitor): Codes for active lac repressor protein constitutively.

• z gene: Codes for β-galactosidase (hydrolyzes lactose to glucose + galactose).

• y gene: Codes for permease (increases cell membrane permeability to lactose).

• a gene: Codes for transacetylase.

• Inducer: Lactose / allolactose binds repressor → repressor inactivated → operator free → RNA pol transcribes operon (negative regulation).

5. Human Genome Project (HGP) & DNA Fingerprinting Workflow

Salient Features of Human Genome

• Total genome size: 3164.7 million base pairs (3.16 × 109 bp).

• Total number of genes: ~30,000 genes (average gene = 3000 bases).

• Largest gene: Dystrophin at 2.4 million bases (X chromosome).

• Gene-rich vs Gene-poor: Chromosome 1 has most (2968 genes), Y has fewest (231 genes).

• < 2% of the genome codes for proteins; repetitive non-coding DNA dominates.

• 99.9% of nucleotide bases are identical in all humans; 1.4 million SNPs ('snips').

DNA Fingerprinting (Alec Jeffreys)

• Identifies differences in repetitive satellite DNA: Variable Number of Tandem Repeats (VNTRs), a type of mini-satellite (0.1–20 kb).

• 6-Step Workflow:

  1. Isolation of genomic DNA
  2. Digestion by restriction endonucleases
  3. Separation of DNA fragments by agarose gel electrophoresis
  4. Southern blotting (transfer) to nitrocellulose or nylon membrane
  5. Hybridisation using radiolabelled VNTR probe
  6. Detection of hybridised DNA bands by autoradiography

• Identical only in monozygotic (identical) twins; used in forensics and paternity disputes.
